## Design brief

### Paved pedestrian path

Replace the stepping stones with a continuous, human-scale paved route to the cabin steps.

Prefer restrained grey concrete pavers with subtle tonal variation, believable joints and a coherent laying pattern. The path should look intentionally built without becoming an oversized suburban driveway.

Inspect terrain, step elevation and actual scene units before choosing width, alignment or grading. Avoid floating pavers, buried edges, sharp unsupported changes in level and grass passing through the paved surface.

Provide modest edge restraint or a credible ground transition where visible. Do not add elaborate landscaping.

### Forest road

First determine whether the scene already contains a usable road or a terrain corridor representing one.

If absent, plan only the bounded road segment needed to explain access. It should read as an unpaved forest road through surface variation, plausible width and continuity beyond the immediate arrival area.

Avoid a disconnected decorative strip ending arbitrarily in grass, excessive road length or unnecessary terrain reconstruction.

### Provisional gravel parking

Place a modest gravel pull-off beside the forest road, connected naturally to the pedestrian path. Use one passenger-car space as the initial sizing assumption unless the existing scene supports a different interpretation more convincingly. State the assumption in the plan.

Express provisional construction through compacted aggregate, irregular but controlled edges and restrained wheel-wear variation. It should remain usable and visually intentional.

No asphalt, painted bays, gates, fences, sheds, signs or vehicle asset are required. Validate car-scale clearance using measurements or temporary planning proxies; exclude those proxies from the final render.

The planner must demonstrate plausible access from the road to the parking area. A car-sized rectangle without an approach is insufficient.
